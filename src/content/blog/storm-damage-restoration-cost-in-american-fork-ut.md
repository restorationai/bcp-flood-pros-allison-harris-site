## How Much Does Storm Damage Restoration Cost in American Fork, UT?

Most storm damage jobs in Utah County run $1,500 to $6,000 for a contained repair (roof, siding, or a single interior area) and $15,000 to $50,000 or more for a job that combines roof replacement, interior water damage, and reconstruction.

| Scenario | Typical cost range |
|---|---|
| Emergency tarping or temporary board-up | $300 - $1,500 |
| Partial roof repair (wind or hail damaged shingles) | $1,500 - $6,000 |
| Full roof replacement after major hail or wind | $8,000 - $20,000+ |
| Interior water intrusion, single room, clean water | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Interior water intrusion, multiple rooms or contaminated water | $5,000 - $15,000+ |
| Full storm loss: roof, interior drying, and reconstruction | $15,000 - $50,000+ |

These are typical industry ranges for the Utah market, not a quote for a specific property. A written, itemized scope after an on-site inspection is the only way to know what a given job actually costs. For the interior-water piece of a storm loss specifically, the cost drivers match what's laid out in [how much water damage restoration costs in Utah](/blog/water-damage-restoration-cost-ut/).

## What Actually Drives the Price Up or Down?

The biggest cost drivers are how much water got inside, how fast the opening was covered, and whether the fix is a patch job or a full [roofing](/services/roofing/) replacement plus [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/).

- **Speed of tarping.** A roof opening covered within hours keeps water out of insulation, drywall, and framing. One left open through a second storm front turns a $2,000 roof repair into a five-figure interior job. [Emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) is usually the first line item on any storm estimate.
- **Type of water intrusion.** A roof leak that starts as rain is usually clean water at first, but it degrades the longer it sits in drywall and insulation, and wind-driven storm rain or surface water flowing in from outside is treated as contaminated water from the start, which changes how much material has to come out versus dry in place.
- **Size and location of the damaged area.** A single bedroom ceiling costs far less than a basement that took on water from a roof failure two floors up.
- **Materials and code upgrades.** Asphalt shingle is cheaper to replace than tile or metal, and a permit-triggered upgrade (ice-and-water shield, updated flashing) adds cost but is often required by current building code.
- **Whether reconstruction is needed.** Cutting out wet drywall and insulation is one price; framing repairs, new drywall, paint, and trim are a separate reconstruction scope layered on top.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover the Repair Cost?

Most Utah homeowners policies cover sudden, storm-caused damage, wind, hail, or a tree limb through the roof, minus your deductible. They typically exclude flood (surface water, rising groundwater, storm surge), which needs a separate flood policy, and they often limit coverage for damage that sat unaddressed for weeks before anyone called it in.

After a storm claim, most insurers either send their own adjuster or ask the restoration contractor to write the repair scope in Xactimate, the line-item estimating software the insurance industry uses to price labor and materials by region. That scope is what gets negotiated against the adjuster's number, which is why a detailed, itemized estimate (not a round-number quote) protects you in a claim. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) has a plain-English breakdown of what a standard policy typically covers, and their [guide to settling claims after a disaster](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) walks through documentation that supports a storm claim: photos before anything is moved, a materials list, and dated moisture readings. Coverage details vary by insurer and policy, so check your own declarations page rather than assuming.

## What Happens During a Storm Damage Restoration Job?

A storm damage job generally moves through five stages, and knowing them helps you read a contractor's estimate instead of just trusting the bottom-line number.

1. **Emergency stabilization.** Tarping the roof, boarding broken windows, and shutting off power to any wet circuits, usually within the same day the damage is reported.
2. **Assessment and estimate.** A walkthrough documents every affected area with photos and moisture readings, then the scope gets written, often in Xactimate, so it matches what an insurer expects to see.
3. **Water extraction and drying.** If water reached the interior, wet materials get removed or dried in place with air movers and dehumidifiers, following the drying principles in the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/).
4. **Exterior repair.** Roofing, siding, gutters, and windows get repaired or replaced once the scope is approved.
5. **Interior reconstruction.** Drywall, insulation, flooring, paint, and trim go back in, with a final walkthrough to confirm the space matches the agreed scope.

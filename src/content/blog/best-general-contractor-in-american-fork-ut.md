## What makes a general contractor the right fit for insurance repair and rebuild work?

The right fit is a contractor who is licensed and insured, writes an estimate the insurer actually recognizes, and pulls the permits without being asked twice. Those three things separate a repair-ready contractor from one who mainly does cosmetic remodels.

Verify any contractor's license status directly with the [Utah Division of Professional Licensing](https://commerce.utah.gov/dopl/) before signing anything. Ask whether they pull permits in-house or expect you to coordinate with the city yourself, since American Fork requires permits for structural, electrical, and plumbing work tied to a rebuild. Building code is based on the [International Code Council's](https://www.iccsafe.org/) model codes, which Utah County adopts and enforces at inspection, so a contractor who hasn't rebuilt to current code before can trigger a failed inspection and a delay.

## How does hiring one contractor for mitigation and reconstruction change the rebuild?

It removes the handoff between the company that dries or cleans up the damage and the company that rebuilds it, which is usually where insurance jobs lose weeks. On a typical claim, a mitigation company documents and dries the loss, then a separate general contractor has to re-inspect the same damage and write its own estimate before work can even start.

When one company carries the job from mitigation through reconstruction, the original scope and photos move forward with it instead of starting over. That's the model behind FIX Restoration's approach: the same estimate the adjuster already reviewed becomes the build scope, and the warranty covers the finished work, not just one phase of it. For a deeper look at how that process works end to end, see [General Contracting & Reconstruction After Damage or Renovation](/blog/general-contracting-reconstruction-renovation/).

## What should you ask a general contractor before signing in American Fork?

Ask for proof of license and insurance, a written scope of work, who is responsible for permits, and the warranty terms in writing before any deposit changes hands. A contractor who can't produce these without hesitation is a red flag, regardless of their online rating.

- Current Utah license status, verified independently through DOPL, not just taken on their word
- A certificate of insurance naming coverage limits
- A written scope tied to either an Xactimate estimate (for insurance work) or a line-item bid (for a standard remodel)
- Who pulls permits and schedules inspections
- Warranty length and what it covers after the final walkthrough

If the job started with a fire, a flood, or storm damage, also ask whether the contractor can start securing the property, such as [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/general-contracting/), the same day you call, before the full rebuild scope is finalized.

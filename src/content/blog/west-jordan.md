## What does a water damage restoration job actually include?

A full water damage restoration job covers four phases: extraction, drying, cleaning, and repair. Skipping any one of them is how a one-day water event turns into a multi-week problem.

- **Emergency water extraction**: Pulling standing water with truck-mounted extractors or portable pumps before it wicks further into flooring, baseboards, and subfloor.
- **Structural drying and dehumidification**: Industrial air movers and LGR dehumidifiers positioned to dry walls, subfloor, and cavities, not just the visible surface.
- **Moisture detection and inspection**: Thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters find water that migrated behind drywall or under flooring, where it won't show up by looking.
- **Repair and reconstruction**: Drywall, flooring, baseboard, and paint brought back to pre-loss condition once everything reads dry.

Commercial properties and flood-level losses follow the same sequence, just scaled up with more extraction equipment and often a longer drying timeline.

## How fast does water need to come out of a home?

Extraction and drying equipment should be running within 24 to 48 hours of the water event, because that's roughly the window before mold becomes a realistic risk on wet materials, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold). Drywall, carpet pad, and untreated wood framing are the first materials to become a problem.

The category of water also changes the timeline. Clean water from a supply line behaves differently than water from a sewer backup or rising groundwater, which is considered contaminated and handled under stricter protocols, per the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). If you're not sure which you're dealing with, that's a reasonable first question to ask whoever you call.

## When is it a job for a professional instead of drying it out yourself?

It's time to call a professional once the water has touched more than a small, contained area, sat for more than a few hours, or come from anything other than a clean supply line. A box fan and an open window dry the surface; they don't dry subfloor or wall cavities.

Sewage backups, groundwater intrusion, and anything involving standing water for more than 24 hours fall outside safe DIY territory; these are treated as contaminated water under IICRC guidelines and need PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and often removal of porous materials rather than just fans. A small, fresh leak you caught within the hour and fully dried within a day is usually fine to handle yourself, provided you check back for hidden moisture over the next few days.

In Utah, fire damage restoration typically runs **$2,500 to $8,000** for a contained, single-room loss and **$60,000 to $150,000 or more** for a whole-structure fire, depending on square footage, how far the smoke traveled, and how much framing needs to be rebuilt. Here is how that range breaks down by loss size, by phase of the job, and by what a homeowners policy is likely to cover.

## How Much Does Fire Damage Restoration Cost by Loss Size?

The cost of a fire restoration job depends almost entirely on how far the fire and smoke traveled, not just how big the flames got. A contained stovetop fire with light smoke staining costs far less to restore than a smoke-only event that worked its way through the HVAC system into every room in the house.

| Loss Type | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Kitchen or stovetop fire, contained to one room, light smoke | $2,500 - $7,000 |
| Smoke-only damage with no direct flame contact (odor and soot throughout) | $3,000 - $12,000 |
| One room burned, smoke spread through the house via HVAC | $8,000 - $25,000 |
| Multi-room fire with some structural charring or framing damage | $25,000 - $60,000 |
| Whole-structure loss, major framing or roof damage, full rebuild | $60,000 - $150,000+ |
| Contents pack-out, cleaning, and short-term storage (billed separately) | $1,500 - $8,000 |

A single-room stovetop fire limited to soot cleaning and odor treatment through a [fire damage restoration](/services/fire-damage-restoration/) call can often stay under $7,000 because the loss never left the kitchen. The moment smoke moves through return air ducts into bedrooms and closets, the job changes from a spot clean to a full-house restoration.

## What Drives the Price of a Fire Restoration Job Up or Down?

Four factors move a fire restoration estimate more than any others: how much square footage the smoke reached, whether firefighting water soaked the structure, the type of smoke residue left behind, and how much framing or roofing needs to be rebuilt.

Smoke type matters more than people expect. Protein smoke from a grease or kitchen fire leaves an almost invisible, hard-to-remove film that discolors paint and metal fast. Synthetic smoke from burning plastics and furniture leaves a thicker, oilier soot that is easier to see but harder to scrub off porous surfaces like drywall and carpet. Restoration crews generally follow the [ANSI/IICRC S700 standard](https://iicrc.org/s700/) for fire and smoke restoration, which spells out how soot, char, and odor get documented and treated based on the type of combustion involved.

Smoke-only damage, even with no flame contact, often needs full-house [smoke damage restoration](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) because soot residue is acidic and keeps etching surfaces if it is not cleaned quickly. Firefighting water adds another layer of cost, since extinguishing a fire usually means extracting and drying hundreds of gallons of water from floors, walls, and insulation before soot cleaning can even start.

## What Does Each Phase of a Fire Restoration Job Cost?

A fire restoration job is really several smaller jobs stacked together: securing the property, cleaning, deodorizing, and rebuilding. Most estimates break down along these lines.

- **Emergency board-up and tarping:** $300 - $1,500. Protects the structure from weather and theft while the claim gets filed.
- **Soot and smoke cleaning** (walls, ceilings, hard surfaces): $1,500 - $15,000, depending on square footage affected.
- **Odor treatment** via thermal fogging, ozone, or hydroxyl generators, typically handled under [odor removal](/services/odor-removal/): $500 - $3,000.
- **Contents pack-out, cleaning, and short-term storage:** $1,500 - $8,000.
- **Reconstruction and rebuild**, often handled under [general contracting](/services/general-contracting/) once the insurance scope is approved: $15,000 - $120,000+, depending on how much framing, drywall, flooring, and paint have to be replaced.

Many fire jobs also need water extraction because firefighting hoses soaked the structure before the flames were out. That portion of the job is priced the same way a standalone flood loss would be; see the breakdown in our guide to [water damage restoration costs in Utah](/blog/water-damage-restoration-cost-ut/) for how that number gets built.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Fire Damage Restoration?

Most standard homeowners policies cover fire damage from sudden, accidental events, including the dwelling, detached structures, and personal contents, up to your policy limits and minus your deductible. That typically includes structural repair, smoke and soot cleaning, and a separate contents claim for damaged belongings, as outlined by the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance).

What a standard policy usually will not cover: a fire set intentionally, damage from an appliance that failed gradually over time rather than suddenly, or any repair cost above your dwelling or contents limits. The [Utah Insurance Department](https://insurance.utah.gov/consumers/home-insurance/home-claims-info/) publishes a plain-language guide to filing a home claim that is worth reading before the adjuster calls.

The deductible is where most homeowners get surprised. A $1,000 or $2,500 deductible on a $4,000 kitchen fire changes the math a lot. Photograph everything before any cleanup starts, keep an itemized list of damaged contents, and get a written scope of work that matches what the adjuster is estimating. That documentation is what keeps a claim moving instead of stalling on disputed line items.

## What Happens During a Fire Damage Restoration Job?

1. **Board-up and tarping** to secure broken windows, doors, and roof openings against weather and theft.
2. **Assessment**, documenting char depth, smoke type, and how far soot traveled through the structure.
3. **Water extraction and drying**, if firefighting hoses soaked flooring, drywall, or insulation.
4. **Soot and smoke cleaning** of salvageable surfaces, fixtures, and contents.
5. **Odor treatment** to neutralize smoke smell at its source, not just mask it with fragrance.
6. **Reconstruction**, framing, drywall, flooring, and paint, to bring the space back to pre-loss condition.

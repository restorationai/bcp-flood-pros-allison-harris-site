## What to Compare When Choosing a Fire Restoration Company

The companies that handle fire damage well in Utah County tend to overlap on five things: how fast they answer the phone, whether they board up and tarp the property the same day, whether they actually run soot and odor remediation (not just a cleaning crew), whether they pack out and store contents during the rebuild, and whether the same company can finish the reconstruction or hands you off to a general contractor.

That last point matters more than homeowners expect. A fire job usually moves through four phases: securing the structure, removing soot and smoke residue, treating odor, and rebuilding what burned. Companies that only do the first two or three end up coordinating with a separate contractor, which adds time and another set of invoices to track.

## How Fire Damage Restoration Actually Works

Fire restoration follows a sequence, not a single visit. Crews typically reference the [ANSI/IICRC S700 standard](https://iicrc.org/s700/) for fire and smoke restoration, the industry benchmark for how soot, odor, and structural cleaning should be sequenced, regardless of which company does the work.

The short version: the property gets boarded or tarped first to stop weather and secondary damage, then soot is removed from hard surfaces and HVAC, then odor is treated with sealing and deodorization (thermal fogging, hydroxyl generators, or ozone depending on severity), and finally damaged drywall, flooring, or framing gets rebuilt. Our full breakdown of each phase is in [the fire damage restoration process, explained](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/), if you want the detail before a crew shows up.

## What Insurance Typically Covers

Most homeowners policies cover sudden fire damage, including smoke and soot cleanup and the structural repairs that follow, though coverage details and deductibles vary by carrier and policy. The [NFPA's home fire safety resources](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/home-fire-safety) are a good starting point for understanding fire risk factors insurers also look at, and Utah homeowners can review how local claims typically get handled through the [Utah Insurance Department's home claims information](https://insurance.utah.gov/consumers/home-insurance/home-claims-info/).

What protects a claim regardless of which company you hire: photographing damage before cleanup starts, keeping a written scope of work, and itemizing any contents that get packed out for cleaning or storage.

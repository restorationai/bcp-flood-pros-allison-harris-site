Most homeowners insurance policies cover sudden wind and storm damage, but the clock starts the moment the storm passes. If you just walked outside after a storm and found shingles in your yard, a fence flattened against your siding, or a tree limb through your garage roof near Canyon Road, you are in the right place. Utah County storms, especially the late-summer and early-fall microbursts that roll down the Wasatch Front, can do serious structural damage in minutes. The question is not whether to act, but how to act in the right order so you protect your home and your insurance claim at the same time.

## What Counts as Storm and Wind Damage?

Storm and wind damage covers any structural harm caused by high winds, hail, falling trees or debris, or the water intrusion that follows when a roof or wall is breached. In Pleasant Grove and the surrounding foothills, the most common scenarios are: missing or lifted roof shingles, damaged soffits and fascia, broken windows from windborne debris, fence and outbuilding collapse, and interior water damage from rain entering through a compromised roof or wall. Hail damage is a separate but related category: it often looks minor on the surface but can compromise a roof's waterproofing layer in ways that cause leaks months later.

The distinction matters for your insurance claim. Damage caused by a sudden storm event is typically covered under the "windstorm" or "hail" peril in a standard HO-3 homeowners policy. Damage that developed gradually, like a slow roof leak that predates the storm, is usually excluded. Documenting the storm event itself (date, time, weather service records) ties your damage to a covered cause.

## What Should You Do in the First Few Hours After Storm Damage?

The first hours after storm damage are the most important for protecting both your property and your insurance claim. Work through these steps in order.

1. **Make sure it is safe to go outside.** Downed power lines, unstable trees, and compromised roof structures are life-safety hazards. If anything looks structurally unsafe, stay inside and call 911 before entering the property.

2. **Document everything before you move anything.** Walk the perimeter and take wide-angle photos and video of every damaged area. Capture the roof from the ground, broken windows, displaced siding, and any debris. Time-stamp your photos. This documentation is the foundation of your insurance claim.

3. **Stop additional damage with emergency protection.** This is your legal obligation under most homeowners policies. Roof tarps over exposed decking, plywood over broken windows, and sandbags or plastic sheeting over open wall sections all count as "reasonable protective measures." Save every receipt. Your policy typically reimburses these costs.

4. **Call your insurance company to open a claim.** Have your policy number ready. Ask specifically about emergency mitigation coverage and whether you need their approval before hiring a restoration contractor.

5. **Call a licensed restoration crew.** A professional assessment identifies damage you cannot see from the ground, like lifted flashing, cracked decking, or compromised attic insulation from rain intrusion. The written scope they produce becomes the working document for your adjuster.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover Storm and Wind Damage in Utah?

Most standard HO-3 homeowners policies in Utah cover sudden wind and hail damage under the windstorm peril. Coverage typically includes: roof repair or replacement (minus your deductible), structural repairs to siding, windows, and doors, and interior damage caused by rain entering through a storm-created opening. What is typically NOT covered: flooding from ground-level water (that requires a separate NFIP flood policy), damage from neglected maintenance, and gradual water intrusion that predates the storm.

Utah County homeowners should also know that some policies carry a separate wind or hail deductible, often calculated as a percentage of the dwelling coverage rather than a flat dollar amount. A 1% wind deductible on a $400,000 home means $4,000 out of pocket before coverage kicks in. Ask your agent to confirm your deductible structure when you open the claim.

For a deeper look at how homeowners policies handle water intrusion specifically, the [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) guide breaks down the covered vs. excluded scenarios in plain language.

FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) is worth knowing about if your property is in or near a flood zone: [fema.gov/flood-insurance](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance).

## What Does the Storm Damage Restoration Process Look Like?

A professional storm damage restoration job moves through several distinct phases. Knowing what each phase involves helps you ask the right questions and understand the timeline.

**Phase 1: Emergency stabilization.** The crew secures the property against further damage. This means roof tarps, [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) for broken windows and doors, and debris removal from areas that create ongoing risk. This phase happens within hours of your call.

**Phase 2: Damage assessment and documentation.** A project manager inspects the full scope of damage, including areas not visible from the ground. Moisture readings are taken inside the structure to identify any water intrusion. The written scope of work produced here is submitted to your insurance adjuster.

**Phase 3: Water mitigation (if rain entered the structure).** If the storm breach allowed water inside, drying must begin immediately. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) defines the protocols for structural drying: air movers, LGR dehumidifiers, and daily moisture monitoring until the structure reaches dry standard. Skipping or rushing this phase leads to mold growth within 24 to 72 hours.

**Phase 4: Structural repairs and reconstruction.** Once the structure is dry and the insurance scope is approved, repairs begin. This includes roofing, siding, drywall, insulation, windows, and any interior finishes affected by water intrusion. A general contracting team handles this phase.

**Phase 5: Final inspection and documentation.** The completed repairs are documented for the insurance file. Moisture readings confirm the structure is back to pre-loss condition.

The full timeline depends on the extent of damage and the pace of the insurance process. Emergency stabilization can happen same-day. Full reconstruction on a significant loss can take several weeks.

## How Do You Choose the Right Storm Damage Restoration Company?

Choosing the right contractor after a storm is one of the most consequential decisions you will make. Storm-chasing contractors, who follow severe weather events and pressure homeowners into signing contracts on the spot, are a real problem in Utah County. Here is what to look for.

**Verify licensing and insurance.** Any contractor performing structural work in Utah must be licensed by the Utah Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing (DOPL). Ask for the license number and verify it at [dopl.utah.gov](https://dopl.utah.gov). Confirm they carry general liability and workers' compensation insurance.

**Look for IICRC certification.** The Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification sets the industry standards for water mitigation and restoration work. An IICRC-certified firm follows documented protocols rather than guessing at drying times or moisture targets.

**Get a written scope before signing anything.** A legitimate restoration company will provide a written scope of work and cost estimate before asking you to sign a contract. Be cautious of anyone who asks you to sign an Assignment of Benefits (AOB) document without explaining exactly what rights you are transferring.

**Ask about direct insurance billing.** Experienced restoration firms work directly with adjusters, submit documentation in the format insurers expect, and can often resolve supplement disputes on your behalf. This saves you from being the go-between.

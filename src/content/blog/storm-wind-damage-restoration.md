## What Should I Do Right After Wind or Storm Damage?

In the first hour after storm damage, your priority is safety, documentation, and stopping additional water from entering. Do not start hauling debris or making repairs until you have photos and video of everything, because your insurance claim depends on that record.

Here is the order that matters:

1. **Stay safe first.** Check for downed power lines, gas smells, or structural instability before walking around the exterior. If anything looks structurally compromised, stay out and call 911.
2. **Document before you touch anything.** Walk the entire exterior and interior. Shoot video continuously, then go back for still photos. Capture the roof from the ground, every broken window, every area of displaced siding, and any interior water intrusion.
3. **Stop active water entry.** Temporary measures like moving belongings away from wet areas and placing buckets are fine. For roof breaches, a professional emergency tarp is safer and more insurance-friendly than a DIY fix. FIX Restoration provides [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) to seal the structure until full restoration begins.
4. **Call your insurance company.** Report the claim as soon as you have documentation. Most homeowners policies require prompt reporting after a storm event.
5. **Call a restoration company.** A certified crew can assess structural damage, check for moisture intrusion with calibrated meters, and provide a written scope your adjuster can work from.

Avoid the temptation to start cleanup before the adjuster or a restoration professional has seen the damage. Premature cleanup can reduce your claim payout.

## What Does Storm and Wind Damage Restoration Actually Involve?

Storm and wind damage restoration is a structured process that addresses both visible structural damage and hidden moisture intrusion. It is not just cleanup, and it is not just roofing. It combines emergency stabilization, water damage mitigation, structural repair, and reconstruction.

**Phase 1: Emergency Stabilization**
The first priority is securing the structure against further damage. This means tarping roof breaches, boarding broken windows and doors, and removing any debris that poses an immediate hazard. This phase often happens the same day as the storm.

**Phase 2: Damage Assessment and Moisture Mapping**
A certified technician walks the property with a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera to find water that has already entered the structure. Wind-driven rain can push water into wall cavities through gaps you cannot see with the naked eye. Catching this moisture early prevents mold growth, which in Utah County's dry climate can still develop within 24 to 72 hours in a saturated wall cavity.

**Phase 3: Water Damage Mitigation**
If water has entered the structure, [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) begins immediately. This means extracting standing water, setting LGR dehumidifiers and air movers, and monitoring moisture levels daily until the structure reaches its dry standard per the [IICRC S500](https://www.iicrc.org) protocol.

**Phase 4: Structural Repair and Reconstruction**
Once the structure is dry and stable, reconstruction begins. This can range from replacing a few courses of shingles to rebuilding a section of roof deck, replacing damaged siding, reframing a wall, or repairing interior drywall and insulation. FIX Restoration's [general contracting](/services/general-contracting/) team handles this phase in-house, which means one point of contact from the first call through the final walkthrough.

**Phase 5: Mold Prevention and Monitoring**
Any storm event that introduced moisture carries mold risk. If moisture was present long enough, [mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/) may be required before reconstruction can close up the walls. This is not optional, and a good restoration contractor will not skip it.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Storm and Wind Damage in Utah?

Most standard homeowners insurance policies in Utah cover sudden wind and storm damage, including roof damage, broken windows, and interior water damage caused by a storm breach. What they typically do not cover is pre-existing deterioration, gradual leaks, or damage caused by neglected maintenance.

Here is what typically matters for your claim:

- **Sudden vs. gradual**: A shingle blown off in a storm is covered. A shingle that had been cracked for two years and finally let water in during a storm is harder to claim.
- **Cause of water entry**: Water damage from a storm breach (roof, window, door) is generally covered. Flooding from ground water or a rising creek is a separate flood insurance matter.
- **Documentation**: The stronger your documentation, the smoother the claim. Photos, video, and a written scope from a licensed restoration company give the adjuster what they need.
- **Deductible**: Some Utah policies carry a separate wind or hail deductible that is higher than the standard deductible. Check your declarations page before the storm season.

## How Do I Know If My Home Has Hidden Storm Damage?

Hidden storm damage is common, and it often does not show up until days or weeks after the event. Watch for these signs in the weeks following a storm:

- **Ceiling stains or bubbling paint**: Water is running down the inside of the structure from a roof breach.
- **Musty smell**: Moisture trapped in wall cavities or attic insulation is starting to grow mold.
- **Soft or spongy drywall**: Drywall that has absorbed water loses structural integrity before it visibly stains.
- **Drafts from windows or exterior walls**: Wind-driven impact may have shifted framing or broken seals.
- **Granules in gutters**: A large volume of asphalt granules after a storm indicates shingle damage that may not be visible from the ground.

If you notice any of these signs, do not wait. The longer moisture sits in a wall cavity or attic, the more expensive the remediation becomes. A moisture assessment from a certified technician costs far less than a full mold remediation job.

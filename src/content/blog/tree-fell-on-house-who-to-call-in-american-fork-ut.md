## Is anyone hurt, and is it safe to go back inside?

Call 911 immediately if anyone is trapped or injured, or if you see a downed power line touching the tree or the house. Treat any line near a fallen tree as live until the utility company confirms otherwise.

Stay out of any room where the tree broke through the ceiling or where you can see daylight through the roof deck. Cracked framing members and a sagging ceiling mean the structure may not hold the tree's weight. Keep kids and pets away from the area, and if you smell gas, get everyone outside and call the gas company from a safe distance before calling anyone else.

## Who do I call first, a tree service or a restoration company?

Call a tree removal company first. They carry the crane, bucket truck, or rigging needed to take the tree off your roof safely, which a restoration crew is not equipped or licensed to do. Once the tree is off, call FIX Restoration at (801) 930-9750, rated 4.9 stars across more than 550 Utah County reviews, to tarp the opening, dry the interior, and start the insurance paperwork.

The two companies do different jobs and neither one does the other's work. A tree service cuts, cables, and hauls away the tree. A restoration company like the one above handles everything that happens to the house once the tree is gone: sealing the hole, pulling wet insulation and drywall before mold sets in, and putting the roof and framing back together. If the opening is small and the tree isn't blocking entry, it's fine to call both at once rather than wait.

## What happens when the restoration crew arrives?

The first visit is about stopping further damage, not starting the rebuild. A technician assesses the size of the opening, tarps and boards it with plywood decking and a weighted tarp system, and photographs every part of the damage before anything gets covered up or removed.

Inside, wet insulation, soaked drywall, and any saturated ceiling material usually come out the same day, since [the EPA notes mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). Air movers and a dehumidifier go in to dry the structure while the roof repair gets scoped. This is the same emergency board-up and tarping process we cover in more detail in our [storm and wind damage guide](/blog/storm-wind-damage-restoration/).

## Does homeowners insurance cover a tree falling on my house?

Most standard homeowners policies cover tree damage caused by wind, since windstorm is a named peril under typical dwelling coverage, subject to your deductible. [Check your own policy for specifics](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance), since coverage details and tree-removal limits vary by insurer and state.

Tree removal itself is sometimes capped at a few hundred dollars unless the tree is blocking a driveway or a covered structure, even when the roof repair is fully covered. And if a neighbor's tree falls on your house, your own policy is usually the one that pays the claim, not the neighbor's, in most straightforward wind events. Document the damage with photos before anything is moved or repaired, since that's what the adjuster will ask for first. ## What does rebuilding the roof and framing involve?

Rebuilding starts once the adjuster has inspected the damage and the tarp is confirmed to be holding. Crews remove the damaged roof decking, rafters, or trusses, then re-deck and reshingle to match the existing roofline, a job covered under [our roofing services](/services/roofing/).

Inside, a [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) crew handles the drywall, insulation, framing repairs, and paint needed to bring the room back to pre-loss condition. A single damaged rafter might close out in a few days. A full truss replacement with interior ceiling rebuild can run several weeks, depending on how much structure the tree actually took out.

Both calls matter: a plumber fixes the pipe, but only fast drying stops warped floors, ruined drywall, and mold, which can start growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours.

## What do you do in the first 60 seconds after a pipe bursts?

Shut off the main water valve before anything else, it's usually near the water meter, in the basement, a utility closet, or an exterior valve box common on American Fork slab homes. Turning this one valve stops the flow at the source faster than closing any single fixture.

If water has reached outlets, light switches, or your electrical panel, cut power at the breaker, but only if you can reach the panel without stepping through standing water. When in doubt, wait for a professional rather than risk shock.

Move rugs, electronics, and anything irreplaceable off the wet floor and up onto dry furniture. Then take a few photos of the water and the source before you touch or remove anything. This single habit makes insurance claims go faster later.

## Who do you call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call both, almost back to back, because they solve different halves of the same problem. A licensed plumber repairs or replaces the burst section of pipe so water stops coming in at all. A water damage restoration company removes the standing water, sets up drying equipment, and documents the loss for your insurance claim, work that needs to start within hours, not days, per the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) for water damage restoration.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after the water stops?

A restoration crew starts with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable pumps before it soaks deeper into subfloor and drywall. From there, moisture mapping with a meter identifies exactly how far the water traveled, often farther than it looks from the surface.

Technicians then set containment if needed, pull baseboards or drill small weep holes in wet drywall to let wall cavities dry, and place air movers and dehumidifiers to start pulling moisture out of the structure. Because a burst supply line is usually Category 1, meaning clean water, materials can often be dried in place rather than torn out, provided drying starts quickly. Left standing for more than a day or two, that same clean water starts breaking down drywall and can turn into a mold problem fast. ## Will homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe in American Fork?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, which a burst pipe usually qualifies as, but they typically deny damage traced to a slow leak, rust, or deferred maintenance, per the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). The distinction your adjuster is looking for is sudden versus gradual.

Keep the photos you took before cleanup started, save any receipts from emergency repairs, and ask your restoration crew for a written scope of the drying work performed. That documentation is what turns a disputed claim into an approved one. Our guide on [whether homeowners insurance covers water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) breaks down what's usually in and out of a standard policy.

Once the plumber has the pipe fixed and drying is underway, the worst of the emergency is behind you. What's left is drying it out completely, which usually takes three to five days of monitored equipment, and then repairing whatever drywall or flooring didn't survive.

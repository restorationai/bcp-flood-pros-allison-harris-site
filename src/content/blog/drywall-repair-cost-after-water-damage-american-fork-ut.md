## How much does drywall repair cost after water damage in American Fork?

Most drywall repairs after a water loss in Utah County fall between $350 for a small patch and $9,000 for a full-room rebuild, depending on how much material was removed and what else has to come out with it. The number moves with square footage, whether the damage reached the ceiling, and whether insulation and flooring went with it.

| Job Scope | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Small patch, single wall, under 10 sq ft (clean water) | $350 - $900 |
| Full wall replacement with flood cut, one room | $1,200 - $2,800 |

These are typical ranges for Utah County rebuild work, not a quote. Every loss is different, and a written scope should come before any demo starts. ## What determines whether you get a patch or a full flood cut?

A flood cut means removing drywall starting well above the visible waterline, usually at least 12 to 24 inches higher, because wet drywall wicks moisture upward past where water visibly touched it. A small, isolated Category 1 (clean water) spot might only need a patch; anything that sat wet for more than a day, or that involved Category 2 or 3 water, usually needs the flood cut.

The decision comes down to three things: how long the drywall stayed wet, what category of water caused it, and whether insulation behind it got saturated. Clean water from a supply line that was caught fast can sometimes be dried in place. Gray or black water, standing water, or anything that sat overnight almost always means the material comes out rather than dries in place, which is standard practice under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) that most Utah restoration and rebuild crews follow.

## What else is part of the rebuild beyond drywall?

Drywall is rarely the only line item. Insulation behind a wet wall almost always gets replaced rather than dried, since wet fiberglass or cellulose loses its R-value and can hold mold spores. Texture matching, most American Fork tract homes use a knockdown or orange-peel texture, adds a separate labor line because blending new texture into existing walls takes a skilled hand to avoid a visible patch line.

Baseboard trim usually gets pulled during the flood cut and either reused or replaced if it swelled. If water reached the subfloor, flooring (especially laminate, LVP, or engineered hardwood) often needs full replacement in the affected room rather than a spot repair, since most flooring doesn't patch invisibly. Kitchen and bathroom cabinets that sat in standing water, particularly particleboard base cabinets common in newer Lehi and Saratoga Springs builds, swell and need replacement rather than drying. Ceiling drywall repairs, which are common after an upstairs bathroom or washing machine leak, cost more than wall repairs because of the overhead labor and the higher likelihood of a full room flood cut.

## Will homeowners insurance pay for drywall replacement after water damage?

Most standard homeowners policies cover drywall, flooring, and cabinet replacement when the underlying water damage was sudden and accidental, like a burst supply line, minus your deductible. They typically do not cover damage from a gradual leak, long-term seepage, or flood water from outside the home, which needs separate flood coverage.

Your adjuster will usually want documentation of the flood cut height, the materials removed, and photos before and during demo, which is exactly the kind of documentation a restoration company should be building into your estimate from day one. For the broader rundown on what a standard Utah homeowners policy does and doesn't cover, see our guide: [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) The [Utah Insurance Department's home claims guidance](https://insurance.utah.gov/consumers/home-insurance/home-claims-info/) is also a useful plain-English reference if you're filing for the first time.

## When do you need a licensed contractor instead of a DIY patch?

A DIY patch makes sense for a small, dry, clean-water spot you can finish in an afternoon. Anything involving a flood cut, electrical boxes in the wall, structural framing, or a ceiling needs a licensed contractor, both for code compliance and because a bad texture or paint match is obvious from across the room.

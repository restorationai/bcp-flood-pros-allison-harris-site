## Is Black Mold More Dangerous Than Other Mold?

Black mold isn't automatically more hazardous than other mold species, but any visible mold growth means there's been a sustained moisture problem long enough for colonies to establish. The [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) doesn't rank mold species by danger level; it treats all indoor mold growth as something to remove and, more importantly, to stop the moisture source causing it.

The color you're seeing tells you less than people assume. A lab test can confirm species, but for most homeowners that's not the deciding factor. What matters is: is it spreading, is it near HVAC returns, and has the moisture source been fixed. If you want a side-by-side on appearance and what it actually means, our breakdown of [black mold versus regular mold](/blog/black-mold-vs-regular-mold/) covers the visual differences people ask about most.

## Who Do You Call First: a Plumber, an Insurer, or a Mold Company?

If water is actively leaking right now, call a plumber first to stop the source, then call a mold remediation company once the leak is shut off. FIX Restoration does not perform plumbing repairs, so that first call matters: fixing the leak and remediating the mold are two separate jobs done in that order.

If the leak already happened weeks or months ago, or the moisture source is condensation, grading, or ventilation rather than an active plumbing failure, you can call a mold remediation company directly for an assessment.

On the insurance side, timing matters too. A sudden, accidental loss, like a burst supply line, is typically covered under a standard homeowners policy, while mold from a slow leak that went unaddressed for a long stretch is often treated differently and may fall under a mold coverage sublimit, if there's coverage at all. Most adjusters want documentation that starts as soon as mitigation begins, not before. The [Insurance Information Institute's overview of standard homeowners coverage](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) is a useful starting point before you call your carrier. ## Mold Inspector or Mold Remediation Company: Who Does What?

A mold inspector tests air and surfaces to confirm what's growing and how far it's spread; a mold remediation company removes it and treats the space afterward. Some jobs need both, in that order; smaller, visually confirmed patches often go straight to remediation without a separate testing step.

If you already see visible growth and know roughly where the moisture came from, a remediation company can usually assess on-site and give you a scope without a separate inspection fee. If the mold is suspected but not visible (a musty smell, no clear source), an inspection or air sample helps locate it before anyone opens a wall.

## What Happens When You Call for Black Mold Removal?

The first call is mostly information-gathering: where the growth is, roughly how large an area, whether the moisture source is known and stopped, and whether you're filing an insurance claim. Have those details ready along with a few photos if you can get them safely.

On-site, the technician measures the affected area, checks moisture content in surrounding materials with a meter, and sets a containment plan. For anything beyond a small, isolated patch, that means sealing the work area with poly sheeting, running negative air pressure, and working under the [ANSI/IICRC S520 mold remediation standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/), the industry benchmark for how containment, removal, and clearance should be handled. Affected porous materials (drywall, insulation, sometimes carpet) typically get removed rather than cleaned, and the space gets HEPA-vacuumed and treated before it's cleared for rebuild.

## When Is a Small Patch DIY, and When Does It Need Containment?

The EPA's general guidance puts the DIY line at roughly 10 square feet, about a 3-by-3-foot area, cleaned with gloves, an N95 mask, and good ventilation, assuming the moisture source is already fixed. Past that size, or if the growth is inside an HVAC system, behind a wall, or you're not confident the moisture source is resolved, it's a containment job, not a wipe-down.

If you're on the fence, a quick phone assessment can usually tell you which side of that line you're on, and whether it's worth scheduling an in-person look.

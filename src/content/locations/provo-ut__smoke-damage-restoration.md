## Why Provo Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

A good share of Provo's housing stock dates to the mid-20th century, with brick exteriors, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and original ductwork that was never designed to filter fine particulate. When a fire starts in one of these older homes, whether from an aging electrical panel or a grease fire, smoke finds its way into wall cavities and attic spaces through gaps that newer construction wouldn't have. Add in the inversion pattern that settles smoke low in the valley during fire season, and homes near Provo Canyon or the benches along the east side of town can pick up smoke odor from fires that never came close to the property line.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Provo

We start by identifying the type of residue, dry smoke from fast, hot fires behaves differently than the greasy, wet smoke left by smoldering synthetic materials, and each requires a different cleaning agent and technique. On brick and masonry exteriors common in older Provo neighborhoods, we test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous section before treating the full surface, since harsh agents can strip mortar joints that have already weathered decades of freeze-thaw cycles. Inside, we work from ceiling down to floor, using HEPA vacuuming and dry sponges on porous materials before moving to wet cleaning, then run air scrubbers to pull residual particulate out of circulation while surfaces are treated. Ductwork gets inspected and cleaned separately, since smoke that's already traveled through an HVAC system will keep redepositing odor into clean rooms if it's skipped.

**Reconstruction after a fire, flood, or storm loss in Draper looks different depending on which side of the bench you live on.** Homes built into the lower slopes near the Traverse Mountains sit on different soil and foundation conditions than the flatter subdivisions closer to I-15, and that difference shapes how we sequence demolition, framing, and permitting once the damage is assessed. FIX Restoration has handled post-damage rebuilds across the Wasatch Front since 2012, and Draper's mix of hillside lots and newer planned communities means no two reconstruction jobs here follow quite the same path.

## Why Draper Properties Need Structural Reconstruction After Damage

Draper grew fast, most of its housing stock dates to the subdivision boom of the 1990s through the 2000s, with newer construction still filling in benches and foothill parcels toward the Traverse Mountains. That growth pattern means a lot of homes share similar truss systems, engineered lumber, and slab-on-grade or daylight basement foundations, which is good news when it comes to sourcing matching materials for a rebuild. The tradeoff is soil: expansive clay and loose fill are common on the benches, and a home that's had water intrusion at the foundation for any length of time may need more than cosmetic repair before we reframe walls or re-hang drywall. Draper also sits close enough to open space and foothill trail corridors that wildland-urban interface exposure is a real factor in fire-damage rebuilds, not just an insurance checkbox.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Draper

We start where the damage assessment and any prior mitigation work left off: removing compromised framing, insulation, and finishes down to sound material, then documenting everything for the adjuster before a single stud goes back up. From there, reconstruction follows the structural engineer's scope if one was involved, local building code for the affected system, and whatever the insurance carrier has approved. Permitting for rebuilds runs through Draper City's building department, and inspections are scheduled at the same framing, rough-in, and final stages as new construction, there's no shortcut just because the work started as a repair. On bench and hillside lots we pay closer attention to foundation moisture and grading before closing up walls, since trapping moisture behind new drywall on expansive soil is how a second, more expensive problem gets created.

## Draper Insurance & HOA Coordination

A large share of Draper's newer subdivisions are HOA-governed, and many of those associations have architectural review requirements that apply even to a like-kind rebuild, exterior paint, roofing material, siding profile, and sometimes landscaping restoration after a fire or storm loss. We coordinate submittals with the HOA alongside the insurance claim so approvals aren't the thing holding up your return-home date. On the carrier side, we document pre-existing conditions, damaged materials, and code-required upgrades separately, since Draper's building department will sometimes require updated electrical or egress work on an older section of a home that wasn't part of the original loss but gets triggered once permits are pulled.

## Local note

On bench-lot homes near the foothills, we've found that post-fire reconstruction often uncovers prior settling cracks in foundations that had nothing to do with the fire, they were just never visible until finishes came off. Flagging those early, before framing starts, keeps the insurance scope conversation separate from any pre-existing structural issue and avoids delays later in the job.
